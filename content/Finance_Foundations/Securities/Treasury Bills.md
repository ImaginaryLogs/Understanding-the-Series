Also known as **T-bills**.

These are [[Debt Securities|debt securities]] issued by the government to finance its operations. Its risk-free rate is a baseline determining the worth of an investment in a given national economy.

## Characteristics:
- **Risk free stable investments** - based on the government to *tax citizens and print money* - virtually no risk of [[default]].
	- **Reinvestment Risk** - if you have an existing 3-month t-bill and it drops in value after 3 months, you are forced to reinvest at a much lower rate next time.
- **Maturity** - short-term, maturing less than one year.
- **Payoff** - mostly low risk
	- Nominal payoff - Near perfect
	- Real return - uncertain as affected by economic inflation.
- **Historical performance** - lowest [[Return|return]] since least [[Risk|risk]], look at [[Historical Evidence|historical evidence]]

# Mechanism behind Risk-free
It is risk-free not only because it is based on trust of the government.
- **Based on Government Trust.** If government stable then risk-free; however, if nation is in a deep-crisis, then investors would doubt the gov.'s ability to return.
- **Based on National Economy.**  Interest rate is adjusted by the gov. on national inflation. 
	- High Inflation, high risk-free rate to compensate investors.
	- Low Inflation, low risk-free rate.
- **Based on Opportunity.** A reflection of the time-value of money in that nation's economy.
	- If a company has cash sitting in the bank, the safest investment they can do is to buy a **treasury bill**.
	- Hence, its is a *baseline against investments*. If an investment project's return is lower than a t-bill, then the better decision is to invest in the latter.

These are [[Simple Discount]] investments.

# US Equivalent
The t-bills of the United States of America are simply **U.S. Treasury Bills** 
- **Issuer** - U.S. Department of the Treasury
- **Common Tenors**: Common maturity end-dates are 4, 8, 13, 17, 26, and 52 weeks.

There are two ways of buying:
- **Direct.** You can buy directly from the US government, such as through **TreasuryDirect.gov**, and you can bid in the same auction as in those in Wall Street every Monday or Tuesday.
- **Indirect.** You can buy through brokerage account. 

Minimum Entry is as low as $100, and its goes in increments in multiples of $100.

# Philippine Equivalent
Local t-bills are called **Philippine Treasury Bills** (T-bills)
- **Issuer** - Issued by Bureau of the Treasury (BTr) on behalf of the government.
- **Common Tenors**: Common maturity end-dates are 91-day, 182-day, and 364 day bills.

However, they are not bought usually over the counter, there is a system:
- **Auction** (Primary Market): Every monday, the BTr holds an auction and large banks (called Government Securities Eligible Dealers) bid on billions of pesos worth of bills.
- **Retail** (Secondary Market): Individual investors buy from banks that won the auction. You can go to a bank like BDO and ask for government securities.

Usually, these institutional auctions involve millions of pesos; however, t-bills can be purchased by retail channels in the Philippines (like the *Bonds.PH* app) as low as $\text{₱} 5,000$.
