Here, we ask what is the value today of cash flow to be **received** in the future.

$$
\text{PV}=\text{FV}_n[\frac{1}{(1 + i_\text{rate})^{n_{\text{years}}}}]
$$


The equation inside the bracket determines the rate at which the future value is valued at - this is the **Present Value Interest Factor** (PVIF)^present-value-interest-factor

| Term               | Name             | Purpose                                                      | Unit                  | Example      |
| ------------------ | ---------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------ | --------------------- | ------------ |
| $\text{FV}_n$      | Future Value     | Predicted value of a compounding investment across some time | Currency              | $1464        |
| $\text{PV}$        | Present Value    | Initial value to place in an investment                      | Currency              | $1000        |
| $i_{\text{rate}}$  | Compounding Rate | Discount applied from an investment                          | Percentage over years | 10% per Year |
| $n_{\text{years}}$ | Term             | Length of compound discount                                  | Years                 | 4            |
The best way to view this is with a [[Timeline Model]], where there is an agreed upon future value and that money is cash-in-advanced back to the present.
